{\sffamily\bfseries\Large Lumbar spine L1--L5 segmentation from CT}

\section{Introduction}
This report presents a pipeline that segments the lumbar vertebrae L1--L5 in CT and locates the centre of each vertebral body (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}). A residual-encoder U-Net (ResEnc U-Net), trained from scratch on TotalSegmentator, segments the vertebrae; a shape rule then extracts the vertebral body. On the TotalSegmentator test split (in-domain) and on VerSe (out-of-domain), the median Dice is 0.97 and 0.94. Most of the remaining error comes from a few scans in which whole vertebrae receive the wrong level name.

\section{Preprocessing and header anomalies (Part A)}
\textbf{Data.} TotalSegmentator v3 (1939 CTs) is used with its official split of 1830 training and 109 test scans. VerSe 2019 (validation and test sets, 80 CTs) is used only for testing, so it remains an unseen dataset. One loader reads both.

\textbf{Preprocessing.} Every scan passes through the same three steps, in training and in inference.
(1)~\emph{Orientation:} image and labels are resampled onto a RAS grid through their affine matrix, which handles any axis order, flip or tilt in one step.
(2)~\emph{Voxel size:} 1.5\,mm in every direction. This is the native voxel size of all TotalSegmentator scans, so the training data are not degraded by resampling. The CT is smoothed before any downsampling; labels use nearest-neighbour interpolation.
(3)~\emph{Intensity:} Hounsfield units (HU) are clipped to a fixed window of $-1000$ to $1500$ and scaled to [0,\,1]. I chose a fixed window over per-scan normalisation because bone then keeps the same value in every scan, regardless of the field of view.

\textbf{Anomalies.} An automatic check reads every header and label file and tests for 30 kinds of problems; Table~\ref{tab:anom} lists those that occurred. Through preprocessing, structure centres move a median of 0.07\,mm (TotalSegmentator) and 0.46\,mm (VerSe), always below 1\,mm; side views of all scans confirm the orientation.

\section{Model and training (Part B)}
\textbf{Network.} The segmentation model is the ResEnc U-Net in its ``M'' configuration (102\,M parameters) from Isensee et al., \emph{nnU-Net Revisited} (MICCAI 2024), trained from scratch. In their benchmark, with all models trained from scratch on the same budget, this CNN outperformed Transformer- and Mamba-based networks. I use only the network, not the full nnU-Net framework: the framework would replace my own preprocessing, and its default mirroring along every axis would swap L1 and L5.

\textbf{Training data.} The network predicts L1--L5 plus T12 and the sacrum; these two extra classes act as landmarks for counting the levels. Training uses crops from T12 to the sacrum with a 3\,cm margin. Crops without a lumbar level, or with only a small piece of one ($<$\,2\,ml), are removed, which leaves 902 training and 100 validation scans. The official test split is not used for any decision. Each patch is 128$\times$128$\times$224 voxels (19$\times$19$\times$34\,cm), large enough to hold T12 to the sacrum, so the network sees the whole lumbar spine at once.

\textbf{Training setup.} The loss is cross-entropy plus Dice. The optimiser uses the nnU-Net defaults unchanged (SGD, learning rate 0.01 with polynomial decay), so no setting was tuned by hand. No data augmentation is applied: flips would swap levels or mirror the anatomy, and about 900 scans suffice for a short run. Training ran for 11\,000 steps (batch size 3, the largest that fits in memory; mixed precision) in 2.6\,h on one RTX~3090. The best validation Dice, 0.905, was reached at the last step. At inference the network runs over the whole scan in overlapping windows; per level only the largest connected piece is kept, and the result is mapped back to the original CT grid.

\section{Vertebral body centroid (Part C)}
\textbf{Method.} The vertebral body is the only part of a vertebra thicker than about 12\,mm; the pedicles, laminae and processes are thinner. The method therefore
(1)~shrinks the vertebra mask by 6\,mm, which removes all thin parts,
(2)~keeps the largest remaining piece that does not lie behind the centre of the vertebra,
(3)~grows this piece back by 6\,mm, but only inside the original mask, and
(4)~takes its centre of mass in world coordinates (RAS, mm), written to one JSON file per scan (Fig.~\ref{fig:body}).
It relies only on the shape of the bone, not on a fixed cutting plane, so it works for any patient position and vertebra size. When nothing survives step~1 (small pieces cut off by the scan edge), the whole-vertebra centre is used instead (28 of 645 vertebrae).

\textbf{Validation.} VerSe provides one annotated point per vertebra. These points lie a median of 11.7\,mm in front of the whole-vertebra centre, so they mark the centre of the \emph{body}. On the VerSe ground-truth masks, the extracted body centre lies a median of \textbf{0.9\,mm} from the annotated point (90th percentile 4.4\,mm), against 12.5\,mm for the whole-vertebra centre. On the predicted masks the distance is 1.0\,mm (90th percentile 5.6\,mm). Only the rule ``not behind the centre'' was added after inspecting VerSe outliers.

\section{Cross-dataset evaluation (Part D)}
\textbf{Label mapping.} TotalSegmentator \texttt{vertebrae\_L1}--\texttt{L5} and VerSe labels 20--24 both map to L1--L5. Transitional vertebrae (VerSe 25 = L6, 28 = T13) keep their own classes, so they are never counted as L5, T12 or background.
\textbf{Scoring.} Dice is computed on the original CT grid, only for levels present in the ground truth (label pieces $<$\,2\,ml excluded). Levels predicted where the ground truth has none are counted separately as false positives. These rules were fixed before evaluation.

\textbf{Differences between the test sets.} The median is lower on VerSe (0.94 vs 0.97), but VerSe voxels do not lie on the 1.5\,mm grid, which alone limits its Dice to 0.96. Relative to these limits, a typical vertebra is segmented about equally well on both datasets, so the domain shift (scanners, fractures, metal) has little effect. The means fall below the medians because a few scans fail as a whole (Fig.~\ref{fig:fail}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Wrong level count} (Fig.~\ref{fig:fail}b, c). In 3 scans (2 TotalSegmentator, 1 VerSe), renaming every predicted level by one raises the Dice by more than 0.1 (e.g.\ from 0.00 to 0.65). The vertebrae are segmented correctly but counted from the wrong start, in short scans without the sacrum or ribs as landmarks, or next to metal.
\item \emph{Cut-off vertebrae} are harder to segment and name. TotalSegmentator labels them (431 scans), VerSe hardly does (8 scans); without them the TotalSegmentator mean rises from 0.89 to 0.92. This explains why the TotalSegmentator mean is the lower one.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Unexpected findings.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{False lumbar vertebrae in scans without a lumbar spine} (Fig.~\ref{fig:fail}d). The model labels 23 whole vertebrae of the chest or neck ($\ge$\,20\,ml; 7 in TotalSegmentator, 16 in VerSe) as L1--L5. It was trained on lumbar crops only and never saw such anatomy.
\item \emph{VerSe leaves vertebrae at the scan edge unlabelled} (Fig.~\ref{fig:fail}a), while TotalSegmentator labels them. In VerSe scans with a lumbar spine, 10 predicted vertebra-sized levels are absent from the ground truth; visual inspection shows real vertebrae.
\item \emph{Patients with L6} (3 VerSe scans) are handled correctly (Dice 0.87--0.95 per level): the model counts from the top, as VerSe does, and leaves L6 unlabelled.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next steps and deliberate omissions}
\textbf{With more resources I would:}
(1)~add patches from whole scans to training, or a ``lumbar spine present?'' check, against the false vertebrae;
(2)~check the level names with a rule (vertebrae ordered from head to feet, with T12 and the sacrum as anchors) against the off-by-one errors;
(3)~train longer, since the best checkpoint was the last one;
(4)~score VerSe only on complete vertebrae, so both datasets follow the same labelling rule, and add confidence intervals, as 67--78 test scans is a small sample.

\textbf{I would deliberately not:}
train or tune on VerSe, because it would then no longer measure transfer to an unseen dataset;
use a larger model to raise the Dice, because the medians are already near their limits and the remaining errors are naming and labelling differences;
train a network for the vertebral body, because no body labels exist and the shape rule is already within 1\,mm of expert points.

\textbf{Code availability.} Code, trained weights and an inference script are provided in \texttt{lumbar/}. The command \texttt{./run\_inference.sh <input\_dir> <output\_dir>} produces a label map and a centroid JSON per CT (Python 3.10--3.12, CPU only, no Docker; tested on Windows~11; 1--3\,min per CT).
